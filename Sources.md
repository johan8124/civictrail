## 1. National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal

Official URL:
https://www.cybercrime.gov.in/

Relevant checklist:
https://www.cybercrime.gov.in/Webform/Crime_AuthoLogin.aspx

Verified facts:
- Incident Date and Time.
- Incident Details with a minimum of 200 characters.
- The incident-details field lists special characters that are not allowed.
- A national ID document can be uploaded in JPEG/JPG/PNG; the checklist states a maximum size of 5 MB.
- For financial fraud:
  - Bank / Wallet / Merchant
  - 12-digit Transaction ID / UTR No.
  - Date of transaction
  - Fraud amount
- Relevant supporting evidence can be uploaded; the checklist states a maximum of 10 MB each.
- The official portal associates financial-fraud reporting with 1930.

Important limitation:
Use these facts specifically for the cyber financial-fraud preparation workflow. Do not present them as universal legal requirements.

### 5.1 National Consumer Helpline (NCH)

Official URLs:
- Portal home: https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/
- About this portal: https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/public/about
- Contact: https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/public/contact

Verified facts (quoted from the About page unless stated otherwise):

1. Operator: the site is headed "Government of India / Department of Consumer
   Affairs / National Consumer Helpline (NCH) 2.0", and states: "This website
   has been launched by the Department of Consumer Affairs to create awareness,
   advise and redress consumer grievances and act as a central registry for
   lodging consumer grievances."
2. Mechanism: "The Department of Consumer Affairs has launched this portal as an
   integrated Grievance Redress Mechanism (INGRAM) for bringing all Stakeholders
   such as Consumers, Central and State Government Agencies, Private Companies,
   Regulators, Ombudsmen and call centres etc. onto a single platform."
3. Online registration is supported: "Consumers can register online their
   grievances through this portal."
4. Character of the service: "This portal is an alternate dispute redressal
   mechanism at pre-litigation level."
5. Escalation: "In case, the consumer is not satisfied, he/she can approach the
   appropriate Consumer Commission."
6. Accepted subject matter: "Grievances related to Consumer Issues can be
   registered."
7. How a grievance is registered: "Any aggrieved consumer can register his / her
   grievance by either calling the toll free number 1800-11-4000 or 1915 and
   talk to an agent or register himself once in the portal, get an userid and
   password and lodge his grievance himself attaching necessary documents, if
   any."
   (CivicTrail reading: documents are described as "necessary documents, if
   any" - the official text does not state any mandatory document.)
8. One-time registration: "A one time registration is required for lodging a
   Grievance. For registration go to the web portal
   http://consumerhelpline.gov.in and click on the login link and then signup
   giving details required, verify through your email. The User id and password
   are created."
9. Other stated channels: SMS on 8800001915; WhatsApp at 8800001915; NCH APP;
   UMANG APP. The home page additionally lists "Multiple ways to lodge
   grievances - Through Online Portal / Through NCH App / Through UMANG App /
   Through WhatsApp", toll free "1915 (8AM to 8PM)" and "WhatsApp
   +918800001915".
10. What happens after submission: "Any grievance received either through online
    registration or call center is entered in the portal and a unique docket
    number is generated and given. Grievances received are sent to the concerned
    company / agency / regulator / ombudsman, as the case maybe, for speedy
    redress. Action taken is updated on real time basis by the concerned agency.
    As a follow up action, these agencies are reminded at stipulated interval."
11. Status tracking: "The status of the Grievance can be tracked from the portal
    under the link "Track your Grievance" without logging into the portal."
12. No guarantee of remedy: "Does registering a grievance in the portal
    guarantee remedy? NCH is a Pre-Litigation stage step. All efforts will be
    made to get the grievance redressed by taking up with the authorities
    concerned. However, if the grievance could not be redressed to the full
    satisfaction, a consumer has choice to approach the appropriate consumer
    Commission."
13. Stated time frame: "It may take up to a maximum of 30 days to arrive at a
    logical conclusion."
14. Current official entry points linked from the home page (2026-09-18):
    register https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/user/index.php ; signup
    https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/user/signup.php ; track
    https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/user/track-complaint.php ; upload documents
    https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/user/upload-docs.php ; knowledge base
    https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/public/knowledgebase
15. Cyber distinction stated in the site footer: "For Grievances Related to
    Cyber Financial Fraud Only Call helpline: 1930".
Important limitations and ambiguities (NCH):

- NCH publishes no mandatory document list. The only document statement found
  is "attaching necessary documents, if any" (About page), plus an "Upload
  Documents" entry point on the home page. Document types, formats and size
  limits are NOT published -> UNKNOWN.
- The "30 days" statement is worded as a possible maximum time "to arrive at a
  logical conclusion". It is NOT a guarantee of remedy and must not be presented
  as a deadline or as a promised outcome.
- Acceptance criteria beyond "Grievances related to Consumer Issues" are not
  specified on the verified pages -> UNKNOWN.
- No fee for registering an NCH grievance is stated on the verified pages.
  Absence of a statement is not evidence that no fee applies -> UNKNOWN.
- The verified pages do not state which company, regulator or ombudsman a
  grievance will be routed to. The About page says it is sent to the
  "concerned company / agency / regulator / ombudsman, as the case maybe" -> the
  routing target is decided by NCH, not by CivicTrail.
- Eligibility rules, limitation periods and grounds for rejection are not
  stated on the verified pages -> UNKNOWN.
- Telephone numbers and channels are as listed on 2026-09-18 and can change.

Safe to use as deterministic product logic (NCH):

- YES: NCH is the Department of Consumer Affairs grievance platform; it accepts
  online registration of consumer grievances; it describes itself as a
  pre-litigation / alternate dispute redressal mechanism; a unique docket number
  is issued; the grievance is forwarded to the concerned company / agency /
  regulator / ombudsman; status can be tracked without logging in; and
  approaching the appropriate Consumer Commission is the officially stated
  option if the consumer is not satisfied.
- YES: no document is stated as mandatory for NCH ("necessary documents, if
  any").
- YES: the NCH site footer routes cyber financial fraud to 1930.
- NO (must not become a deterministic rule or claim): mandatory document lists,
  fees, eligibility, timelines as guarantees, recovery expectations, or any
  statement that NCH will resolve the dispute.
### 5.2 e-Jagriti

Official URLs:
- Portal: https://e-jagriti.gov.in/
- FAQ: https://e-jagriti.gov.in/faq
- Contact (Commission addresses): https://e-jagriti.gov.in/contact-us
- Help documents: https://e-jagriti.gov.in/help-docs
- Help videos: https://e-jagriti.gov.in/help-videos

Verified facts (quoted from the official pages as rendered on 2026-09-18):

1. Who presents it: the header reads "DEPARTMENT OF CONSUMER AFFAIRS, MINISTRY
   OF CONSUMER AFFAIRS, FOOD & PUBLIC DISTRIBUTION | GOVERNMENT OF INDIA", the
   brand line is "e - Justice And Grievance Redressal through Information
   Technology and Innovation", and the footer states the site is "designed,
   developed and maintained by National Informatics Centre, Ministry of
   Electronics & Information Technology, Government of India".
2. Online complaint filing (FAQ 1): "Consumers can file an online complaint
   before the Consumer Commission by following the simple steps provided under
   the HELP DOCS/HELP VIDEOS section on the e-Jagriti Platform. These resources
   contain detailed, step-by-step guidance for filing a complaint and uploading
   the necessary documents."
3. Onboarding steps (home page): "1 Register - Register to create an account.
   2 Profile - Login and create your profile. 3 Complaint - File a complaint."
4. Mandatory affidavit (FAQ 4): "Yes, submission of a Notarized affidavit is
   mandatory while filing a complaint before the Consumer Commission. The
   affidavit should be duly signed and notarized, confirming that the facts
   stated in the complaint are true and correct to the best of the complainant's
   knowledge."
5. Advocate not required (FAQ 3): "No, hiring an advocate is not mandatory for
   filing or representing a Complaint before the Consumer Commission. Consumers
   can present and fight their own Complaint without the assistance of an
   advocate. However, if desired, a consumer may engage an advocate to represent
   them during proceedings."
6. Filing fee (FAQ 2): "The filing fee depends on the value of goods or services
   paid as consideration under Consumer Protection Act 2019, and the level of
   Consumer Commission where the case is being filed." Stated table:
   District Commission - up to Rs 5,00,000: Nil; above Rs 5,00,000 and up to
   Rs 10,00,000: Rs 200; above Rs 10,00,000 and up to Rs 20,00,000: Rs 400;
   above Rs 20,00,000 and up to Rs 50,00,000: Rs 1,000.
   State Commission - above Rs 50,00,000 and up to Rs 1,00,00,000: Rs 2,000;
   above Rs 1,00,00,000 and up to Rs 2,00,00,000: Rs 2,500.
   National Commission - above Rs 2,00,00,000 and up to Rs 4,00,00,000:
   Rs 3,000; up to Rs 6,00,00,000: Rs 4,000; up to Rs 8,00,00,000: Rs 5,000;
   up to Rs 10,00,00,000: Rs 6,000; above Rs 10,00,00,000: Rs 7,500.
   (The portal writes these amounts with the rupee sign; they are recorded above
   without it.)
7. Status tracking (FAQ 5): "You can check your Complaint status through the
   'CASE HISTORY/STATUS' tab available on the e-Jagriti website. Simply enter
   your Reference Number or Case Number to view the latest updates and
   proceedings."
8. Filing for someone else (FAQ 8): "Yes, Consumer can file a complaint on
   behalf of your relative or friend, provided you have their written
   authorization (vakalatnama or authorization letter) to represent them before
   the Consumer Commission."
9. Judgment copies (FAQ 9): "Consumer can download Judgment/Order copy directly
   from the e-Jagriti Platform under the 'Case History/Status' OR Judgment
   section. However, if you require a certified copy of the judgment, you will
   need to contact the concerned Consumer Commission where the Complaint was
   heard."
10. Document formats (FAQ 10): "For the exact and updated formats of mandatory
    documents (such as affidavits, memo of parties and synopsis etc.), you are
    advised to contact the concerned Consumer Commission directly. They can
    provide the prescribed specimen or format as accepted by that Commission."
11. Hearing mode (FAQ 6): "The National Commission and All State Commissions are
    conducted the hearing in Hybrid Mode (Online and Physically both mode are
    available). The District Commission conducted the hearing depends upon the
    concerned Consumer Commission." Complainants and opposite parties are advised
    to check the hearing mode for the respective Commission in the Causelist.
12. Forum location (FAQ 7): "Yes, Consumer Commissions are established in almost
    all districts across India. You can check the details of your District
    Consumer Commission by visiting the following link:
    https://e-jagriti.gov.in/contact-us" - that page lists National Commission
    (NCDRC), State Commission (SCDRC) and District Commission (DCDRC) contact
    details.
13. Official support (FAQ 5 and site footer): helpdesk numbers 011-2430-5702,
    011-2430-5703, 011-2430-5728, 011-2430-5752, 011-2430-5766 and email
    helpdesk-ejagriti[at]nic[dot]in; the footer states availability "Available
    on All Government Working Days" 9:30 AM to 5:30 PM.
14. Platform sections offered (navigation): CASE DETAILS, CASE HISTORY/STATUS,
    JUDGEMENT, DISPLAY BOARD, CAUSELIST, LOGIN / SIGN UP.
15. Help resources exist as first-party files: /help-docs lists 14 filing-flow
    documents including "2. Consumer Complaint (CC) Case Filing"
    (https://e-jagriti.gov.in/static/media/CC_Filing.34e0fc5dd03b1d864cab.pdf)
    and "1. Onboarding"
    (https://e-jagriti.gov.in/static/media/Onboarding_Tutorial.9e89535dbbd244dc2691.pdf);
    /help-videos lists tutorials including "Consumer Complaint (CC) Case
    Filing".

### 5.3 Routing distinctions that affect CivicTrail

Supported by the sources above:

- NCH is the grievance/redress platform at pre-litigation level; e-Jagriti is
  the route for filing a complaint before a Consumer Commission. NCH's own About
  page states that a consumer who is not satisfied "can approach the appropriate
  Consumer Commission".
- Cyber financial fraud is not routed to NCH: the NCH site footer states "For
  Grievances Related to Cyber Financial Fraud Only Call helpline: 1930", and
  cybercrime.gov.in remains the verified cyber route (section 1 above).
- Neither route may be presented as a guarantee of remedy.

UNKNOWN (unstated by the official sources - must not be used as product logic):

- Whether NCH requires any specific document or information beyond the account
  details implied by the one-time registration.
- The complete set of documents required for an e-Jagriti complaint, and their
  prescribed formats (the official source defers to the concerned Commission).
- Any limitation period or deadline for filing under either route.
- Whether a fee applies to registering an NCH grievance.
- Which authority NCH forwards a specific grievance to.
- Any promised resolution, timeline guarantee or recovery expectation.
